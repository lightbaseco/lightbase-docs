Code might describe only part of your data model:
* Services reach databases through aliases and environment variables whose values never appear in the source.
* A column no query touches is invisible to indexing.
* ...
In **Databases and tables** curation you can fill those gaps.

You can declare the databases you work with, mark replicas and review the tables Lightbase discovered while [indexing](/architecture/indexing-process). Merging duplicates, and editing schemas down to the column.

## Manage your databases

Define the databases that matter and describe them in the terms your team uses.

* **Name, alias, and engine.** The logical name your services use, alongside the real one.
* **Host and description.** Enough context that anyone following the data lineage knows which system it is.
* **Replication.** Mark a database as a replica of another, so reads against the replica trace back to the same data as the primary.

## Refine down to the column

A curated table is editable at field level.

* Column **names and types**, plus **primary key** and **nullable** flags.
* **Foreign keys** pointing at other curated tables, so relationships survive even when the database never declared them.
* A **category** per column, and as many **categories** as you need per table. Categories are free-text tags such as `sensitive` or `financial`. They follow the data through every flow that reads it.
* The **column mapping** per service, so a rename in one place keeps the link to the rest.

## Upload a DDL

Upload the DDL of a database and Lightbase reconciles it with what it observed in code. You get the columns no query ever referenced, real types, and the constraints the database enforces, merged into the tables you already curated.

<Tip>
  A DDL is the fastest way to curate a large, mature schema. Lightbase fills in the structure so you only curate naming and business meaning.
</Tip>
