After [indexing](/architecture/indexing-process), Lightbase holds an inferred model of your code, your infrastructure, and your data. Data curation is the tool for manually editing that model, so it matches the way your company groups and names internal business concepts.

From there, both the diagrams Lightbase draws and the answers it gives use your naming and your structure.

## What curation changes

Diagrams, chat answers, blast radius, and [data lineage](/mcp/data-lineage) all read from the same model, so an edit here shows up in every one of them.

| Inferred by indexing                                           | After you curate it                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| `cust`, `customers`, and `tbl_client` in three services        | One table, with each service's columns mapped to it     |
| A users table and the six normalized tables around it          | A single `Users` business entity                        |
| An application named after the repository it was found in      | The service name your team uses, inside its own group   |
| Three endpoints reaching the same vendor                       | One counterparty group                                  |

## Automatic code updates

Manual edits always take precedence over inference. Nothing you curate is overwritten by a later analysis.

When new code arrives — a new table, a new service, a new external call — Lightbase integrates it into the model with its best guess and flags it for review, so a human can confirm the guess or replace it. Newly discovered tables collect in **Tables pending assignment**, and you always know what has arrived since you last looked.

<Info>
  Everything you curate is internally marked as curated, so your semantics are always distinguishable from what Lightbase inferred.
</Info>
