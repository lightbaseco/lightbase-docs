---
title: "Business entities"
description: "Group strongly related tables under the concepts your domain has words for."
---

A **business entity** groups tables that hold a strong relationship with each other. `Users` is an entity: the users table plus every normalized table hanging off it — profiles, credentials, addresses, preferences, consents. Your domain has one word for all of that, and a business entity is where that word lives.

Entities are not limited to one database or one service. When the same concept is stored by several applications, a single entity covers all of it, so the concept answers as one thing no matter how many places it is written to.

## Why they change the answers you get

Without entities, every question about your business is really a question about tables, and you need the schema in your head before you can ask anything.

## Lightbase proposes, your domain decides

Lightbase generates a first set of entities from your code and your curated schema. That set is a proposal by design: entity vocabulary is the most company-specific thing in any system. One organization's *customer* is another's *account*, and a third calls it a *party* because that is the word its regulator uses.

| You want to…                                            | Do this                                |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Add a concept that only exists in your domain language  | Create an entity                       |
| Match a proposed entity to the term your teams use      | Rename it                              |
| Make an entity's meaning explicit rather than inferred  | Add a description                      |
| Attach the tables that make up the concept              | Assign tables one by one, or in bulk   |
| Split or regroup a concept as your model evolves        | Reassign tables between entities       |

<Tip>
  Start with the ten or twenty entities that carry your business. Coverage matters less than getting the core vocabulary right — the long tail can wait until someone needs it.
</Tip>
