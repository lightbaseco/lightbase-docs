title: "Applications"
description: "Set the vocabulary, grouping, and scope of the applications your model is built around."
---

**Applications** are the unit Lightbase reasons about. A flow starts at an entrypoint in one application, crosses into another over the network or a queue, and ends up writing to a database owned by a third. Every cross-service answer is phrased in terms of applications, so their names and their groupings are yours to define.

[Detection](/architecture/indexing-process) takes its names from repositories and manifests, which is rarely how your organization refers to its services. In **Applications** you set that vocabulary, and the scope the model covers.

| You want to…                                                            | Do this                        |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Use the name your team says out loud                                    | Rename the application         |
| Report flows and impact at product, domain, or team level               | Add it to an application group |
| Resolve which database or endpoint its configuration points at          | Set its environment variables  |
| Drop a deprecated service or a false positive out of the model          | Disable the application        |
| Connect a producer and a consumer that spell the same topic differently | Curate its message queues      |

## Rename

Rename an application to whatever your team calls it in Slack and in incident channels. The name propagates everywhere the application appears — flows, lineage, blast radius — so answers stop needing a translation step.

## Application groups

Group applications into the units you plan work around: a product, a domain, a platform, a team's surface area. Grouping lets Lightbase roll flows and impact up to that level, so a blast radius reports "three services in Payments" instead of listing nine repositories.

## Environment variables

Applications reach their databases and their peers through configuration, not hardcoded addresses. Supply the values behind those variables per environment and Lightbase resolves which [curated database](/data-curation/databases) or service endpoint a `DATABASE_URL` really points at. That is what turns an inferred connection into a confirmed one.

## Enable and disable

Disable an application to take it out of the model without changing what you index. It is the right move for a deprecated service, a folder of generated code, or a false positive from application detection.

<Note>
  Nothing is lost. The repository stays indexed, and enabling the application brings it back.
</Note>

## Message queues

Queues are one of the interactions Lightbase traces, and they are where cross-service flows usually break, because a producer and a consumer rarely agree on how to spell a topic.

* **Name a queue or topic** in the terms your architecture documents use.
* **Merge naming variants**, so one logical queue stops appearing as several.
* **Confirm producers and consumers**, joining both ends into a single traceable flow.

## Why it pays off

Applications are the backbone of every cross-service answer Lightbase gives. Curating them is cheap — mostly renaming and grouping — and it is what makes flows, lineage, and blast radius readable to people who did not write the code.
